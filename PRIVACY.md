# Mile Marker Privacy Policy

_Last updated: 20 Aug 2026_

Mile Marker is a private, offline-first trip-tracking app. This policy
covers what the app accesses, what it stores, and — most importantly —
what it does **not** do with any of it.

## What Mile Marker collects

- **Location.** Mile Marker uses your device's GPS to draw the route of
  trips you record, to auto-detect when you start driving, and to place
  photo pins and comments at the right spot on the map. This includes
  background location access, used only while a trip is being recorded or
  while automatic driving-detection is turned on in Settings — never
  collected for any other purpose.
- **Photos.** If you attach a photo to a trip (from your camera or photo
  library), Mile Marker stores a copy of that photo on your device so it's
  attached to that trip permanently, even if the original is later deleted
  from your photo library.
- **Notes and reviews.** Any trip name, end-of-trip review, or mid-trip
  comment you write is stored with that trip.

## Where it's stored

Everything above is stored **only on your device**, in a local database
and local files. Mile Marker has no backend server and no account system —
there is nothing for us to collect, because nothing ever leaves your
phone unless you explicitly export it yourself (Settings → Export Data),
which creates a file you choose where to save or send.

## Who it's shared with

Nobody. Mile Marker does not transmit any location, photo, or trip data
to any server, developer, or third party. There is no analytics SDK, no
ad network, and no crash-reporting service that phones home with your
data.

## Notifications

Mile Marker can send local notifications (trip auto-detected, long stop
detected) — these are generated and delivered entirely on-device and are
not routed through any push-notification service.

## Your control

You can revoke location, camera, photo library, or notification
permissions at any time in your device's system settings; the
corresponding features will simply stop working rather than silently
retrying. Deleting the app deletes all of its local data.
